Kavyakusumanjali — An Offering of Flowers from the Garden of Sanskrit Poetry.

Sanskrit literature is an immense ocean of poetic beauty, wisdom and imagination. Scattered across its mahakavyas and other great works are countless passages that linger in the mind — vivid descriptions of nature, tender moments of human emotion, episodes of nobility and devotion, and reflections on the many shades of life.

This collection is an attempt to gather some of these exquisite passages into a single garland. It brings together selected sargas and episodes from different works and poets: Rama's lyrical evocation of the rainy season and the beauty of autumn in the Kishkindha Kanda; Dileepa's encounter with Nandini in Kalidasa's Raghuvamsha; and many such other jewels drawn from the rich treasury of Sanskrit poetry.

The selections are eclectic, guided not by chronology or completeness, but by an appreciation of literary excellence, poetic charm and the enduring beauty of expression. Each piece is intended to stand on its own, while together they offer a glimpse into the extraordinary range and splendour of the Sanskrit literary tradition.

It is, in essence, a personal anthology — a garland woven from the finest flowers of Sanskrit kavya.
