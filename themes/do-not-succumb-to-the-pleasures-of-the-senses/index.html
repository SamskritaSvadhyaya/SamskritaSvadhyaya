Do not succumb to the pleasures of the senses

Bhagavad Geeta · The Story of Hanuman

ॐ

Bhagavad Geeta

Chapter 2 · Shloka 2.58 →

यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः ।
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥2.58॥

Simple meaning: And when this person completely withdraws the senses from sense objects from all sides, just as a tortoise draws in its limbs, his wisdom is firmly established.

Chapter 2 · Shloka 2.59 →

विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः ।
रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥2.59॥

Simple meaning: For an embodied person who abstains from sense objects, the objects turn away, leaving behind the subtle longing for them; even that inner longing ceases upon realizing the Supreme.

Chapter 2 · Shloka 2.70 →

आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् ।
तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्‍नोति न कामकामी ॥2.70॥

Simple meaning: Just as waters enter the ocean—which, though constantly being filled from all sides, remains unmoving in its stillness—so too, he into whom all desires enter without disturbing him attains peace, and not the one who craves desires.

The Story of Hanuman

Yuddhakanda · Sarga 128 · Shloka 6.128.93 →

सर्वात्मना पर्यनुनीयमानो यदा न सौमित्रिरुपैति योगम्।
नियुज्यमानोऽपि च यौवराज्ये ततोऽभ्यषिञ्चद्भरतं महात्मा॥6.128.93॥

Simple meaning: When Lakshmana — though earnestly persuaded in every way to be appointed as the crown prince – did not accept the position, then the great-souled Rama anointed Bharata as the crown prince.
