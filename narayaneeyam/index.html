Narayaneeyam

नारायणीयम् · Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri · 100 dashakas

ॐ

A Short Summary

Narayaneeyam is a devotional Sanskrit poem of 1,036 verses divided into 100 dashakas (cantos), composed by Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri in the late 16th century. It is a condensed retelling of the Shrimad Bhagavatam, especially emphasizing devotion (bhakti) to Lord Krishna as the Supreme Being.

The work begins by describing the supreme, transcendental nature of Narayana, the creation of the universe, and the various incarnations of Vishnu. It then narrates the lives of great devotees and sages before devoting nearly half the work to Krishna's life, from His birth, childhood sports in Gokula and Vrindavana, lifting Govardhana, subduing various asuras, the Rasa-lila, His life in Mathura and Dwaraka, and many episodes from the Tenth Canto of the Bhagavatam.

Throughout the poem, Bhattathiri interweaves philosophical teachings of Vedanta with intense devotion, presenting Krishna as both the Supreme Brahman and the most lovable personal deity. The final dasakas culminate in a magnificent meditation on the beautiful form of Guruvayurappan (Krishna of Guruvayoor), concluding with the poet's heartfelt prayer for devotion, spiritual liberation, and freedom from disease.

Traditionally, the Narayaneeyam is recited as an act of devotion, and it is especially revered at the Guruvayoor Temple in Kerala. According to tradition, Bhattathiri composed it while suffering from severe rheumatism, offering one dashaka each day to Lord Guruvayurappan, and is believed to have regained his health upon completing the hundredth dashaka.

Story of how Guruvayoor came to be

According to tradition, at the end of Dvapara Yuga, when Dwaraka was about to be submerged, Uddhava was inconsolable, saying that if Krishna goes away, who will guide the world. Then Krishna asks Uddhava to take the sacred idol of Lord Vishnu that had been worshipped in Vaikuntha and later by Vasudeva and Krishna Himself. Krishna entrusted the idol to Brihaspati (Guru), the preceptor of the Devas, asking him to find a suitable place in the South and establish it for worship.

Guru, accompanied by his disciple Vayu, the Lord of Winds, journeyed southward and, guided by Lord Parasurama, reached a beautiful, verdant spot beside a lotus-filled lake. There they installed the sacred idol. The place came to be known as Guruvayoor, combining the names of Guru and Vayu. “Oor” means city. Because the deity came from Dwaraka and continued the worship of Lord Krishna, Guruvayoor is revered as the "Dwaraka of the South."
